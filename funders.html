Who pays for coordinating the El Niño response

Hundreds of millions of dollars are lined up for acting early on this El Niño. Very little of it pays for the work that makes those funds fit together: convening the actors, sharing data, aligning triggers and money, giving local actors a say, and learning what worked. This page lists who funds that coordination work, what they pay for, how to reach them, and which problems on the problem map each one touches.

What this means

Money for coordination is small and concentrated. Most of it comes from a few government donors, above all Germany's Federal Foreign Office, and reaches the field through a handful of platforms: the Anticipation Hub, REAP, Start Network, OCHA and the Global Shield. Almost none of it is open to outside applicants. A facilitation or platform group gets funded by becoming useful to one of these platforms and being written into its programme, not by applying to the large pooled funds.

Open tools are the exception: NLnet and FLOSS/fund take direct applications from individuals. Event money such as CERF, the FAO–WFP appeal and the risk pools is listed for context, because it is what coordination is meant to line up.

How to read this

  • Fit says how a coordination effort can reach the money: directly, through a partner organisation, or not at all (event money, shown for context).
  • Function is the kind of coordination work the funder pays for. One funder can cover several.
  • Problem links are the author's reading of each funder's stated purpose, not a statement by the funder.
  • VERIFY marks figures or terms not checked against a primary document.

Missing or wrong?
