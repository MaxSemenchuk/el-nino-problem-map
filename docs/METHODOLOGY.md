# Methodology

The map answers one question: **what stands between a very strong El Niño forecast and effective collective action, and who is working on each obstacle?**

It borrows the structure of a problem inventory first built for a different complex, high-uncertainty domain (organising elections under war). The structure is domain-independent: a layered list of problems, each scored the same way, linked to the organisations that work on it.

## Layers

| Layer | What it covers |
|---|---|
| 0 Frame | What success means, the event path, the time window |
| 1 Forecast and knowledge | Forecast skill, translating climate signals into impacts |
| 2 Triggers and decision rules | When and how to act on a forecast |
| 3 Finance | Funding, pooled funds, insurance, fiscal exposure |
| 4 Delivery and operations | Readiness, last mile, targeting, local actors |
| 5 Data and information | Early-warning systems, surveys, baselines, interoperability |
| 6 Sector impacts | Food, livestock, health, water and energy, ecosystems, displacement |
| 7 Governance and coordination | Mandates, ownership, accountability, legitimacy |
| 8 Communication and trust | Hype, misinformation, reach, probabilistic messaging |
| 9 Political economy | Will, compounding crises, politicisation, incentives |
| X Cross-cutting | Problems that belong to no single layer |

## Labels

- **STRUCTURAL**: the problem exists regardless of this event. It is a chronic gap.
- **EVENT**: created or sharply amplified by the 2026–27 El Niño.
- **VERIFY**: a fact in the entry needs a primary-source check. Shown as a dashed ring in the graph.

A problem can carry both STRUCTURAL and EVENT only if its structural cause and its amplification are both significant. None does at present.

## Constraint class

- **H** hard: physics, logistics, time. Cannot be changed by decision.
- **I** institutional: rules, mandates, funding mechanisms. Can be changed, slowly.
- **P** political: interests and incentives. Can be shifted, not decreed.

## Nature of uncertainty

The point of the map is to reduce uncertainty, and different uncertainties need different tools.

| Code | Nature | Reduced by |
|---|---|---|
| F | Factual: a number or norm that is unknown but can be found | Research and verification |
| S | Scenario: depends on how the climate event unfolds | Not reducible by us; plan for several scenarios |
| G | Strategic: depends on choices of other actors | Commitments, shared rules, understanding incentives |
| N | Normative: a question of values with no correct answer | A legitimate process of choosing, for example facilitated deliberation |
| T | Structural or technical: a known trade-off or unsolved engineering problem | Choosing which distortion is acceptable; new data will not help |
| R | Random event: type known, timing and size not | Robust design and contingency plans |

## Degree of uncertainty (1 to 3)

1. Known, a path to a solution exists.
2. Partly known.
3. Unknown or not knowable now.

## Impact (1 to 4)

How much the problem determines whether early action works in time and reaches the people at risk.

- 4 Critical: failure here makes early action fail or arrive too late.
- 3 High.
- 2 Moderate.
- 1 Low.

## Organisations and link kinds

- `active`: public sources show the organisation working on the problem.
- `partial`: related work, or a role that was not confirmed in detail. Usually also marked `"v": true`.
- `planned`: a proposed niche for the facilitation and platform group. These are hypotheses, not a claim about what exists.

“Nobody found” in the gaps view means no organisation was found in the public sources collected, not that nobody is working on it.

## Limits

- Scores are the author's judgement, made once. They are for discussion.
- The organisation list is not exhaustive and skews towards international and humanitarian actors. National and local actors are under-represented.
- Facts were collected from public web sources on the as-of date and are not independently verified. See `SOURCES.md`.
